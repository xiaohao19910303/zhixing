# When do people experience mental health conditions for the first time?

## Knowing when people are most at risk allows us to offer support when it might make the biggest difference.

Mental health conditions can begin at almost any age. For many people, they develop in the early stages of life.

Around a third of people who will ever experience a mental health disorder do so by the age of 14.1 Two-thirds do by the age of 25.

But this varies a lot by condition. Some are much more common in childhood or adolescence, while others are more evenly distributed across adulthood. Knowing the age at which people might be particularly vulnerable to the onset of a mental health condition is important. It helps societies offer support — whether that’s an assessment of risk factors, screening, or preventive interventions — at the right time to make a difference.

The chart below shows how this age distribution looks for nine different mental health disorders. Each curve shows, among people who will ever develop that condition, the share who first do so at any given age.

The data behind this comes from a large meta-analysis of 192 studies by Marco Solmi and colleagues.1

Around a third of people who will ever experience a mental health disorder do so by the age of 14.

I’ve sorted these conditions into three groups.

First, those that **commonly develop at any point in someone’s life**. This includes generalized anxiety disorder, depression, and bipolar disorder (the latter two are often referred to as mood disorders).

Second, those that tend to **develop earlier in life, with a very strong age peak around early adolescence**. Social anxiety tends to first develop in childhood, and almost 80% of those who will experience it at some point in their lives do so before the age of 18. Eating disorders tend to begin during someone’s teenage years, with a peak age of 16 and a median age of 18.2

And third, those that tend to **peak around age 20 but will often develop for the first time throughout adulthood**. This includes substance use (alcohol and cannabis) disorders and schizophrenia, which commonly develop in someone’s twenties.
